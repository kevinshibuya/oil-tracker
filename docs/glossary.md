# Glossary

Seeded from the design session. Plain words first, precision second.

- **AIS**: the radio beacon every large ship broadcasts with position, speed, heading and a few
  crew-typed fields such as destination and draught. Terrestrial receivers cover coasts; the open
  ocean needs satellites.
- **Aframax, Suezmax, VLCC**: crude tanker size classes, roughly 700 thousand, 1 million and 2 million
  barrels. VLCC stands for very large crude carrier.
- **Product tanker (MR, LR1, LR2)**: carries refined fuels such as diesel and gasoline, not crude.
- **Cargo ship**: containers or dry bulk. Never oil.
- **Draught**: how deep the hull sits in the water. Heavier means deeper. In AIS it is typed by the
  crew and often stale.
- **Deadweight (DWT)**: the most a ship can carry, in tonnes, when sunk to its deepest legal line,
  fuel and water included.
- **Scantling draught**: the deepest the hull may legally sit. Full deadweight corresponds to it.
- **Tonnes per centimetre (TPC)**: the weight it takes to push the hull one centimetre deeper.
- **Laden, ballast**: loaded, empty. A tanker in ballast carries seawater for stability and pumps it
  out while cargo comes aboard, which is why draught barely changes during loading.
- **Load factor**: how full a ship actually left, as a fraction of deadweight. Near one for mid-size
  classes at Ingleside, about two thirds for a VLCC the channel depth will not let fill.
- **Deadweight scale**: the method of reading cargo from how far below the deepest line the ship
  still sits, using tonnes per centimetre.
- **Partial load**: a VLCC that leaves less than full because the channel is too shallow.
- **Reverse lightering**: smaller tankers top up a VLCC offshore because it could not fill at berth.
- **STS transfer**: ship-to-ship cargo transfer at sea.
- **Dark ship, going dark**: AIS switched off.
- **Port call, lifting, fixture**: a vessel's visit, the act of loading, the charter contract.
- **Floating-roof tank**: the roof rests on the liquid, so its shadow depth in imagery shows the fill.
  This measures inventory, not production.
- **Nowcast**: an estimate of the current period before the official figure publishes.
- **Rolling-origin scoring**: each month is scored with parameters fitted only on earlier months.
- **SAR**: synthetic aperture radar. Sentinel-1 imagery that sees ships day or night, through cloud.
- **CFAR**: constant false alarm rate, the standard simple detector for bright targets in radar images.
- **Sentinel-1, Sentinel-2**: the European radar and optical satellites, free through Copernicus.
- **EEZ**: exclusive economic zone, 200 nautical miles out; the free US AIS archive stops there.
- **HS 2709**: the customs code for crude petroleum.
- **EIA, Census, PortWatch**: the US energy statistics agency, the US customs trade data source,
  and the IMF's free port-call dataset.
